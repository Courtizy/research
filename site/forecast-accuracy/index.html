The question

A DCF's first five years usually start from the company's own history: recent growth, fading toward the economy. How accurate is that, and does the model's range around it (Bear to Bull, P10 to P90) really contain the outcome four times in five?

Hypotheses, set before looking

  1. H1. Extrapolating past growth over-forecasts revenue for the fastest growers, because growth reverts to the mean.
  2. H2. For operating margin, holding last year's margin is at least as accurate as averaging recent years.
  3. H3. The model's P10–P90 revenue range covers less than 80% of outcomes, and the shortfall grows with the horizon.

Findings

    What it can't tell you

    • It tests mechanical forecasts built only from filings. Analysts and management know more; their forecasts aren't scored here.
    • Associations, not causes. A sector or growth group that forecasts badly isn't shown to be badly forecast because of the sector or the growth.
    • Large US filers only (revenue of $1B or more in a cohort year), so small and private companies may behave differently.

    Data

    • Source: SEC EDGAR XBRL company facts (US government data), fetched with a contact User-Agent within the SEC's fair-access limits.
    • Universe: the largest filers by revenue (at least $1B) in calendar 2011, 2015 and 2019, taken together. Firms that were later acquired or stopped filing stay in, so the sample isn't only survivors.
    • Excluded: finance (SIC 60–67), where revenue isn't a comparable measure.

    Point in time, both ways

    Each forecast origin is the day a 10-K was filed. The Valuation Model's own normalizer runs as of that day, so only facts filed by then count. Each actual is that year's revenue as first reported in its own 10-K, so restatements made later never leak into either side. A test re-runs every origin with the later filings deleted and requires identical forecasts.

    The forecasts

    MethodRevenue h years out
    No changeLast year's revenue. The baseline every method has to beat.
    Own historyLast year's revenue grown at the company's three-year growth rate.
    SectorGrown at the median three-year growth of other companies in the same SIC division that year.
    ModelThe Valuation Model's default DCF case: growth from the company's history (capped −5% to 20%), fading to 2.5% over ten years. Its P10–P90 range comes from the model's simulation (near-term growth ±3 points, triangular), computed exactly.

    Scoring

    • Error = ln(forecast ÷ actual). Symmetric and scale-free.
    • Bias and skill use errors winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles; medians use raw errors.
    • Standard errors are clustered by company, since one company's overlapping origins share its surprises.
    • Companies need at least three years of history at the origin to be scored.

    Validation

    • No look-ahead: forecasts identical with later filings removed (test)
    • Actuals as first reported, not restated (test)
    • The range matches a 40,000-run seeded simulation of the model's draw (test)
    • The fade matches the engine's own projection code (test)
    • Deterministic: the same inputs give the same results (test)
